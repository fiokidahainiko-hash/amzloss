Amazon Associates Missing Commission: Why & How to Check

You sent the traffic, the shopper bought, and then the commission didn't show up — or showed up smaller than expected. Before you assume Amazon made an error, know that most "missing" commission has a mundane explanation. In 2026 there are also new ones, like the halo change on April 14. Here are the eight most common reasons and how to verify each one from your own report.

The 8 most common reasons

  1. The 24-hour window expired. Amazon's cookie lasts 24 hours from the click. If the shopper bought after that, no commission.
  2. Returned or cancelled. Commission reverses when the order is returned or cancelled.
  3. Shipping-only or gift-card orders. Purchases that are only shipping, or paid entirely with gift cards, often earn nothing.
  4. Boosted or paid ads. Since April 14, purchases referred through paid or boosted advertisements linking to Amazon don't qualify (with limited exceptions).
  5. The halo change. Since April 14, you earn only on the exact ASIN you linked. Items the shopper added to their cart that you never linked no longer pay.
  6. Not settled yet. Amazon pays roughly 60 days after the earning month. Recent orders simply aren't paid yet.
  7. A rate change with no changelog. 2026 rate cuts reportedly ran up to 50% without a public category list, so a line can pay less than the rate at referral time.
  8. Reporting lag or detail removed. Reduced item-level reporting can make a line harder to spot, not absent — which is why checking the CSV matters.

How to tell "missing" from "not paid yet"

Open your Product Earnings Report in Associates Central. An order earns when it's "shipped" (or "settled"), and appears in the report for that period. If the order shows but with a smaller amount than expected, check the current rate for that category. If the order doesn't show at all and it's within ~60 days, it's likely just unsettled — not missing.

How to verify with a real check

  1. Export the CSV. Reports → Product Earnings Report → download for the period in question.
  2. Run an audit. Our free audit parses the file entirely in your browser and compares each line against the correct 2026 rate, separating returns and ambiguous rows from real flags.
  3. Total the shortfall. The audit groups flagged lines by category so you can see where commission genuinely went missing.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Amazon commission missing?

The most common reasons: the 24-hour click window expired, the order was returned or cancelled, the purchase was a gift card or shipping-only, the click came from a boosted or paid ad (disqualified since April 14), or the shopper bought items you didn't link — which stopped earning after the halo change.

Does Amazon pay commission on returns?

No. When an order is returned or cancelled, the commission for that order is reversed and shows as a negative adjustment or disappears from later reports.

How long does Amazon take to pay?

Amazon pays commission roughly 60 days after the month in which the order was earned. A recent order may simply not have settled yet — that's not "missing."

Does Amazon pay on items I didn't link to?

Not since April 14, 2026. Amazon now pays onsite commission only on the same ASIN variant as the product you linked — the halo commission on the rest of the cart is gone.

How do I find missing commission?

Download your Product Earnings Report CSV and audit it. Compare each line's commission against the correct rate for its category and order date, then total any shortfall. An AmzLoss audit does this in your browser.
