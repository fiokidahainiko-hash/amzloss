Deep Linking: Send Buyers Straight to the Product

🔗Free tool:Link Tools — build clean affiliate links and check backlinks.

If every Amazon link on your site drops readers on the homepage, you're paying a heavy conversion tax. A reader who has to re-find the product you already told them about can bounce or search a competitor instead. Deep linking — pointing directly at the product page, with your tag on it — is the single biggest conversion lever in an affiliate's link setup. It's also the one Amazon itself expects, and it's easy to get right once you know the anatomy of the URL.

Why the homepage link is a trap

Linking to amazon.com with your tag does technically work — Amazon tracks the click and credits a purchase within the session window. But between the click and the product page there's a search, a scroll, and a moment for the reader to change their mind. Every extra step costs conversions. A deep link to https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0EXAMPLE123?tag=yourstore-20 drops the buyer straight onto the exact product, so the click and the purchase are separated by one "Add to cart" decision instead of a whole shopping trip. For the same effort, deep links routinely convert measurably better — and readers trust a link that goes where you said it would.

Building a deep link correctly

The correct deep link is the product's /dp/ASIN URL with your tracking ID and nothing else:

https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0EXAMPLE123?tag=yourstore-20

Three details decide whether it works. The ASIN is the product's permanent ID — grab it from the product page, not from a search URL, because search URLs can break. Your tag must be the first parameter and use your marketplace's suffix (-20 US, -21 UK). And drop every ref= parameter Amazon added when you copied the URL; they don't help and can bury your tag. Deep linking beyond product pages

Product pages are the obvious target, but the same logic applies to category pages and deal pages. Linking a category page in a "best of" roundup is legitimate and can be smarter than one product if your recommendation is really a range. What you should never do is deep-link to a page that isn't yours, like a login or a search-results page with your tag — Amazon treats that as gaming and it's a quick way to lose the account. Keep deep links to real, shoppable pages and they work as intended.

Protecting deep links after the fact

A deep link only converts if it keeps working. Products go out of stock, get delisted, and move marketplaces — all of which silently turn your perfect link into a homepage redirect and a lost sale. Make link checking part of your editorial process: when you update a post, re-test each deep link, and use your Associates traffic report to spot URLs that stopped getting clicks. Frequently asked questions

What is deep linking in Amazon Associates?

Deep linking means sending a reader directly to a specific Amazon page — a product's /dp/ASIN page, a category, or a deal — instead of the Amazon homepage. It uses your tracking ID on the standard URL, which is exactly what Amazon's operating agreement requires; the link stays a normal direct link with your tag.

Do deep links increase conversions?

Yes. A reader who lands on the exact product you reviewed is already past the search step, so the click is far more likely to end in a purchase than one landing on a homepage where they have to find the item again. Fewer steps between your page and the basket means a higher click-through rate.

Why is my deep link redirecting to the homepage?

Usually because the ASIN in the URL is wrong, out of stock, or no longer listed, so Amazon falls back to its homepage. It can also happen if the product is not available in the marketplace your link points to. Double-check the ASIN and the marketplace suffix, and test the link before publishing.
