How to Verify Your Amazon Affiliate Commission

🔍Free tool:Earnings Audit — check your Amazon earnings report for underpayment.

Here's the uncomfortable truth about Amazon Associates in 2026: the reported numbers can be short, and the reporting itself gives you less detail to spot it with. Amazon doesn't make mistakes loudly. Rates change quietly, category mappings shift, and the monthly "you earned this" figure is usually accepted at face value. Verifying your commission isn't paranoia — it's a five-minute check that either confirms your payouts are correct or hands you the exact evidence to dispute them.

What verification actually means

Verifying a commission means comparing what you were paid against what the rate in effect says you should have been paid, line by line, using the order date. That date matters more than anything: Amazon's rates changed partway through 2026, so a 4% line from January might be correct while the same category at 4% in July is a shortfall. You can't verify against today's rate table alone — you need the rate history for the period you're checking.

That's where the Current Rates page and the Earnings Audit come in. The audit knows the rate that applied to each order date, reads every paid line from your Product Earnings Report CSV, and flags anything that paid below the correct rate. If you're doing it by hand, the columns guide shows which fields carry the rate, the fee, and the commission so you can do the same comparison in a spreadsheet.

Which columns tell you the truth

Your earnings report doesn't spell out "this line is wrong." You have to read it. The fields that matter for verification are the order date, the product category, the advertised and effective rates, and the paid commission. Two red flags to know:

  • Paid rate below the advertised rate. The advertised rate is the headline; the effective rate is what actually applied. If the effective rate sits below the advertised rate for your market and category on that date, that's your shortfall.
  • Commission that doesn't match the math. Multiply the eligible order amount by the effective rate. If the paid commission is less, the line is underpaid — regardless of what the rate column says.

The audit automates exactly this check, so you don't have to eyeball thousands of rows. It also groups shortfalls by category and period, which tells you whether the problem is one bad month or a systematic pattern across everything.

Understand exactly what you're owed

When the audit flags lines, it totals the difference per order and across the report. That total is what you're owed — the concrete figure to build a claim around. Before you contact Amazon, sanity-check two things. First, exclude adjustments: a return or an item swap from a previous month can legitimately reduce a line, so cross-reference flagged orders against your order activity. Second, confirm the rate history is right for your market — the audit uses the published per-market rates, and a claim on the wrong rate is a claim you'll lose.

If everything checks out, you're looking at a clear, documented underpayment. The next step is filing a claim — and the claim guide walks through exactly how to present your evidence so Amazon's support team can act on it.

Frequently asked questions

What does the earnings audit check?

The audit reads every paid line in your Product Earnings Report CSV and compares the commission you were paid against the correct rate for that order date and category. It flags anything that comes up short and totals the difference, so you know exactly what you're owed.

Is my data uploaded when I run the audit?

No. The audit runs entirely in your browser — your CSV never leaves your machine and is never stored or uploaded anywhere. That's also why the file stays out of your browser history when you follow the in-page instructions.

What if the audit shows no issues?

That's the best possible result. It means every paid line matches the rate in effect for its order date, and you have a clean record to rely on. Keep your latest report CSV saved as your baseline for the next period.
