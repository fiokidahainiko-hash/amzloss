Backlinks for an Affiliate Site: 30-Day Plan

Free tool:Backlink Directory — get a free, relevant backlink.

Almost every affiliate site has the same first-year story: the content is good, the commission rates are decent, but the traffic never quite arrives. The missing ingredient is almost always links. Affiliate sites don't get links by accident — they get them by running a deliberate, repeatable process. This is that process, condensed into a 30-day plan you can start today. It's built around the four methods that actually work for affiliate publishers: directories, guest posts, roundups, and link-worthy content.

Weeks 1–2: The foundation (directories and quick wins)

Start with the links that are fastest to earn. Curated directories take ten minutes per submission and give you a layer of relevance signals you can build on. The free directories guide explains which ones are worth it in 2026 and which to skip — the short version is: only moderated, niche-relevant directories. The AmzLoss Backlink Directory is a good first submission because it's aimed specifically at affiliate sites and reviews every listing by hand.

In the same two weeks, claim the basics that many publishers forget:

  • Local and industry profiles where your niche naturally lives (review-site profiles, author bios on platforms you already use).
  • Existing partners. Email the brands and tools you already mention and ask if they link out to partner sites. You'd be surprised how often the answer is yes.
  • Fix your own site. Internal links and a clean sitemap don't build external links, but they make the ones you earn pass more value.

Weeks 3–4: Guest posts and roundups

Now move to the higher-value work. Guest posts on niche blogs still work, but the rules matter: pitch a specific topic (not "write for your blog?"), include genuinely useful information, and never stuff a link in awkwardly. A guest post should earn you one contextual link and one new audience, not a link farm slot.

Roundups are the underrated workhorse of affiliate link building. Every niche has bloggers who publish "best of" lists — best budget espresso machines, best standing desks, best tools for freelancers. If you have a genuinely good page in their niche, a one-line email with a link to your best content works far more often than people expect. Track which of your pages are strongest (the Earnings Audit helps here by showing which products actually convert), and pitch those specific pages instead of your homepage.

Make one piece of content that earns links on its own

The four-week rhythm keeps links coming, but link-worthy content is what turns a trickle into a flow. Affiliate sites win with data: a survey of your audience, an original price-comparison dataset, a breakdown of shipping costs across retailers, a calculator that's actually useful. These get picked up because they save other people work. Publish one of these per quarter and point the guest posts and roundup pitches at it — a single strong piece can earn more links in a year than everything else combined.

One warning as you go: keep the pace sustainable. Building hundreds of links in a week is a red flag. Ten to fifteen relevant links a month, steadily, is a pace search engines trust. Directories are your fastest start, but they're the appetizer, not the meal.

Frequently asked questions

How many backlinks does an affiliate site need?

There's no fixed number, but a young affiliate site with no links should aim for a steady, sustainable flow rather than a sudden spike. Ten to fifteen relevant, quality links a month is a healthy pace; the sites that get hurt are the ones that build hundreds of junk links overnight.

What are the best backlinks for affiliate sites?

The best links are contextual and editorial: guest posts on niche blogs, mentions in industry roundups, links from quality directories, and links earned by genuinely useful content. All of these are relevant to your niche, which matters more than raw link count.

Do backlinks from directories count?

Yes, when the directory is moderated and relevant to your niche. Curated directories still pass value, and they're one of the easiest quality links for a new affiliate site to earn. Spam directories that auto-approve everything don't count for much and can even look suspicious.
