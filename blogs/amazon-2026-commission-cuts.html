What the 2026 Amazon Commission Cuts Mean for Affiliates

📊Free tool:Current Rate Table — see the live 2026 rates by category and market.

In 2026, publishers reported Amazon Associates commission cuts of up to 50% in some categories, the removal of milestone-based bonuses, and reduced reporting visibility. Many found out months later — through smaller payouts, not announcements. If you're an Amazon affiliate, this is the most significant rate change since the April 2020 cuts, and it's different in one crucial way: this time there is no reliable public changelog telling you exactly what changed.

This guide covers the headline changes, which categories were hit, why it matters more than 2020, and — most importantly — how to measure exactly what the cuts cost your account and what to do about it.

The headline changes

Across the Amazon Associates program, the 2026 changes reported by affiliates and publishers include:

  • Rates cut up to 50% in some publisher categories (reported drops such as 10% → 4–5%).
  • Milestone bonuses removed — the volume-based bonuses that rewarded top-performing affiliates were discontinued.
  • Reporting reduced — less item-level detail and weaker API access, so verifying each sale became harder.

These three changes compound. A lower rate on the same sales is bad enough. Remove the bonus structure that made high-volume sites viable, and then take away the reporting detail affiliates use to notice — and you have an environment where earnings drift downward silently.

Why this is worse than the 2020 cuts

In April 2020 Amazon cut rates across major categories — furniture from 8% to 3%, grocery from 5% to 1%, and similar reductions elsewhere — with announced, publicized changes. Affiliates knew within days, updated their expectations, and adjusted strategy.

The 2026 changes were reported to have rolled out quietly. No single announcement told publishers exactly which categories changed, by how much, or on which dates. Instead, affiliates discovered the impact in their own numbers — a smaller monthly payment, a lower rate on a report line, or a bonus that simply never arrived.

That's precisely the situation where money gets lost silently. Without a changelog to compare against, "did the rate change, or did my traffic shift?" becomes impossible to answer from memory. It's a question only your own transaction data can answer.

Which categories were hit

Because there is no official public changelog, the practical source of truth is your own Product Earnings Report. The categories affected reportedly include several physical-product lines where rates fell from double digits to the 4–5% range. Higher-paying lines like Luxury Beauty and Amazon Games appear less affected in reported data — but "less affected" is not "unaffected."

The lesson for content strategy: before you write another product review, verify the current commission rate for that product's category. Our current rate table shows 2026 rates against the pre-cut rate, so you can see at a glance which categories are now marginal.

How to measure the hit on your account

The only reliable way to quantify the 2026 cuts is to compare what you were actually paid against what the published rates should have produced for your order dates. That means:

  1. Export your data. In Amazon Associates Central, go to Reports → Product Earnings Report and download the CSV for the affected period.
  2. Run an earnings audit. Our free earnings audit parses the file entirely in your browser, compares each line against the correct 2026 rate, and flags every line where the paid commission is below what the rate should produce.
  3. Total the shortfall. The audit groups flagged lines and shows which categories hold the biggest possible underpayment, so you know where to focus.

Because everything runs locally, nothing is uploaded. There is no account, no sign-up, and no risk to your earnings data.

What to do next

  1. Run the audit now. It takes about two minutes and gives you a number, not a guess.
  2. Prioritise high-value categories. If a category you promoted dropped from 10% to 4%, re-evaluate whether that content is still worth the SEO effort.
  3. Diversify your programs. Amazon is the baseline, not the whole income. Add higher-paying or recurring affiliate programs in the same niches so one rate cut can't sink your month.
  4. Track your earnings-per-click (EPC). If EPC falls while clicks stay flat, that's your early warning that a rate change (or tracking change) hit your account.

Frequently asked questions

When did the 2026 Amazon commission cuts happen?

Changes rolled out during 2026 without a single public announcement. The practical detection method is comparing your paid commissions against the correct rate for your order dates — which is exactly what an AmzLoss audit does.

Did Amazon announce the commission changes?

Reportedly no — not with a clear category-by-category changelog. This is why measuring your own account matters more than ever.

Will Amazon raise rates back?

There is no announced schedule. Historically, Amazon has not walked back category cuts once made. Assume the current rates are the new baseline and plan content accordingly.
