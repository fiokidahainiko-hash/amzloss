Amazon Associates Claim Report: How to File

🔍Free tool:Earnings Audit — check your Amazon earnings report for underpayment.

The 2026 Amazon Associates reporting changes left a lot of publishers staring at smaller payouts and thinner data. If you've confirmed your commission is short — by checking paid lines against the correct rate — the next step is filing a claim. Most publishers skip this step because it feels like shouting into a void. It isn't. Claims get resolved every day, and the difference between a generic "everything looks fine" reply and an actual correction is usually preparation. Here's how to file one that gets read.

Know when a claim is justified

A claim isn't a hunch, it's a discrepancy. You should be able to point at specific orders and say "this paid at 4% when the category rate was 6% on that date." Do that verification before you write anything. The verification guide walks through the check, and the Earnings Audit does it for you automatically — flagging every paid line that falls short of the correct rate for its order date. Use that output as the backbone of your claim.

Also check the timing. Amazon Associates reports a qualifying order in the month it was filed, and commission can be adjusted for returns and other corrections. A single line being short is sometimes a return adjusting a previous month. That's not a claim — that's accounting. A claim is for systematic underpayment: many lines, same category, consistently below the published rate.

Build your evidence before you write

Amazon's support team answers faster when you hand them specifics. Before opening a ticket, assemble:

  • The affected rows. Copy the relevant lines from your Product Earnings Report CSV (order IDs, dates, category, paid rate).
  • Your expected math. For each row, the rate you expected and what the commission should have been.
  • A short summary. "12 orders in category X between June 1–15 paid at 4% instead of the 6% rate in effect — total shortfall $42.16."

If you haven't pulled the report yet, the columns guide explains exactly which columns carry the rate and commission data you need.

Wording that gets results

Amazon's associates support is a human team handling hundreds of tickets a day. Make their job easy. Use this shape for your message:

  • Subject line that states the issue: "Commission discrepancy — category X orders underpaid in June 2026."
  • First sentence says what you want: "I'm requesting a review of 12 orders that appear to be paid below the applicable rate."
  • Middle gives the evidence: the rows, the expected rate, the shortfall per order and in total.
  • Close with the ask and a timestamp: "Please confirm the correct rate for these order dates and adjust the affected commissions."

Keep it factual and never accusatory. "The paid rate appears to differ from the rate in effect" gets a much better outcome than "you're stealing from me." Attach the CSV or a screenshot on the first message — don't wait to be asked.

What to expect next

You'll normally get a first response within a few business days, though busy periods can push it to two weeks. The first reply is often a template asking you to confirm details — that's normal, not a rejection. Reply once with confirmation, and if there's no real answer after two weeks, follow up politely on the same ticket. Track your ticket number, because starting a new ticket resets the conversation. If the discrepancy is real, expect either a corrected commission on your next statement or a detailed explanation of why the amount was right — both are outcomes you can work with.

Frequently asked questions

How do I file a claim with Amazon Associates?

Log in to the Associates console, open the Contact Us page under the Help menu, choose the category that matches your issue, and attach your evidence — a copy of your Product Earnings Report CSV or the relevant rows. Explain the discrepancy in plain terms with order dates and expected versus paid amounts.

How long does Amazon take to answer a claim?

Responses usually arrive within a few business days, though high-volume periods can stretch to two weeks. If the first reply is a generic template, reply once to push for a specific answer. Follow up politely if there's no response after two weeks.

Do I need proof before contacting Amazon?

Yes — attach your evidence on the first message. Amazon's support team works from the data in your account, but a clear summary of the specific orders, dates, and the amounts you expected vs. what was paid makes your case dramatically easier to act on.
