How to Submit a URL to Search Engines (Free)

Free tool:URL Submitter — ping search engines and IndexNow for free.

Publishing a page and waiting for Google to find it can mean days of silence. Submitting the URL doesn't guarantee a ranking, but it removes the waiting from your control: a page submitted through the right channels gets crawled in hours instead of weeks. There are only two submission channels that matter in 2026 — Google Search Console and IndexNow — plus a pile of legacy services that no longer do anything. Here's what to use, in order, and what to skip.

The only two channels that matter

Every modern search engine accepts discovery through one of two mechanisms. Google uses Search Console's URL Inspection tool: paste a live URL, click "Request indexing," and Google crawls it — typically within a few minutes to a day. Microsoft Bing, Yandex, and the other IndexNow partners use a protocol called IndexNow: you submit the URL plus a site key, and they crawl within hours. That's it. Between those two, you cover essentially every engine people actually use. The old XML-RPC ping directories and "submit to 10,000 engines" services are dead weight in 2026 — none of the major engines act on them, and several of those forms just harvest your email.

Submitting to Google correctly

Set up Google Search Console first — it needs your verified site in the property list. Then, to request indexing: open URL Inspection, paste the exact final URL (including https:// and any trailing slash), press Enter, and click "Request indexing" once the inspection finishes. A few practical notes: requesting indexing on a page that's already indexed does nothing, so it's a new-page tool. It's also rate-limited, so don't submit a whole site — submit the one or two pages that matter most, and let your sitemap carry the rest. If a page shows "Indexed" in Search Console but won't rank, that's a content or relevance problem, not a submission problem.

Submitting via IndexNow

IndexNow works differently and is genuinely fast: Bing and its partners promise crawling within minutes to hours of a valid submission. You need a key — a random string hosted at yourdomain.com/{key}.txt so the engines can verify you own the domain — then you send a simple HTTP call with your URLs. It sounds technical, but it's a two-step setup, covered step by step in our IndexNow setup guide. Once the key is in place, submitting a new URL takes one request, and doing it on every publish keeps your pages fresh in Bing, Yandex, and the rest before their next scheduled crawl.

Automate it so you never forget

Submission only helps if it happens on every publish, and manual consistency fails. That's what the URL Submitter is for: paste a URL once and it fires the Google Search Console request and the IndexNow ping for you, then remembers what you've submitted so you don't double-request the same page. Pair it with a short pre-publish checklist — the one in our submission checklist covers title, meta, sitemap, and internal links — and every new page gets its crawl request the moment it goes live.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth submitting URLs to search engines?

Yes, but with realistic expectations. Submission is a nudge, not a guarantee — Google finds good pages on its own, but IndexNow speeds up Bing, Yandex, and other partners from days to hours, and Search Console's URL Inspection tells you exactly when and why a page was indexed or not. Submitting is free and takes seconds, so the only cost is doing it consistently.

What is IndexNow and who uses it?

IndexNow is a simple open protocol that tells participating search engines about new or changed URLs instantly. Microsoft Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver and others use it. You submit a URL plus a key to prove you own the site, and the engines crawl it within minutes to hours instead of waiting for their next scheduled crawl.

Do ping services still matter in 2026?

Mostly no. The old XML-RPC ping services that alerted blog directories are effectively dead, and none of the major search engines act on them anymore. The useful modern equivalent is an IndexNow ping against the engines that support it, plus a URL Inspection request in Google Search Console. Skip the legacy ping directories.
