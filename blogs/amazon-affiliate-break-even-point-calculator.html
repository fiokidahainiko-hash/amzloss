An affiliate post earns commission only once its revenue covers the time and effort it took to publish. That point — the break-even — is easy to estimate and surprisingly motivating: it tells you which articles are working and which are quietly costing you money.

What break-even means for a blog post

Break-even is the point where net commission from a post equals the value of the hours you spent on it. Below that, the post loses money even though it "pays". Above it, the post compounds. For a reviewer spending 6 hours per article, the break-even requires enough sessions, at a known conversion and commission, to cover those 6 hours.

The inputs that decide it

Four numbers drive the calculation:

  • Category rate — the correct 2026 rate for the product you review.
  • Average order value — what a converted shopper actually spends, post-discount.
  • Conversion rate — the realistic share of visitors who buy through your link (2-5% is a sane range for review content; buy-intent posts can run higher).
  • Content cost — your hourly value × research, writing, images and refresh time.

With these, sessions-to-break-even = content cost ÷ (conversion × commission per sale).

Why 2026 changed the number

In home and furniture the rate dropped to 1%, so commission per sale fell by roughly 70% on big-ticket items — and the break-even sessions shot up by the same factor. A post that paid back at 2,000 sessions a month may now need more than 6,000. That is a planning number worth knowing before you invest more hours in that category.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my break-even sessions?

Divide your content cost by (conversion rate × commission per sale). For 6 hours of work, a 3% conversion and €4.50 per sale, break-even is roughly 6 × hourly value ÷ 0.135 sessions.

What conversion rate should I assume?

2-5% for review content and 5-10% for strong buy-intent comparison posts. Audit your own historical rates and use those instead of an industry guess.

Did break-even get harder in 2026?

Yes, in the cut categories. Home and electronics now require roughly 3x the sessions to break even, which is exactly why the pre-write calculator matters more this year.
