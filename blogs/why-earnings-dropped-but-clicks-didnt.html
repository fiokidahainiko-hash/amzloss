Why Your Earnings Dropped But Your Clicks Didn't

Your traffic is steady. Your clicks are steady. But your commissions shrank. That's not "a seasonal dip" — that's your earnings per click changing. When clicks hold and money drops, something structural changed in how much each click is worth. It's almost always one of three things, and the good news is you can tell them apart in about fifteen minutes.

Start with the metric that matters: EPC

Earnings-per-click (EPC) is your total commission divided by your clicks. It's the single most useful number for diagnosing a silent earnings drop, because it removes traffic as a variable. If clicks are flat and EPC is down, the problem is in the value of each click — not in your content or your traffic. Here's what causes EPC to fall.

1. A commission rate cut

Amazon changes category rates periodically — most recently in 2026, with cuts reported up to 50% in some categories. If the products you promote sit in a cut category, the same number of sales now earns less, and your EPC falls across the board. The 2026 changes were notably quiet: there was no clean public changelog telling you what moved. Check the current rate table against the products you push, then compare your paid commission to what the rate should have produced.

2. Category drift

You might link to a kitchen item (4.5%) but the buyer purchases from a lower-rate category in the same cart. Because Amazon pays on qualifying items in the session — not just the linked product — buyers' extra purchases can quietly pull your blended rate down. Over months of "cart stacking," your effective rate drifts lower than the category you think you're promoting. The fix is to see what you're actually being paid per category, not what you assume you're earning.

3. Tracking or reporting changes

If commissions genuinely vanish for clicks you know converted, check for missing tracking tags on your links and broken redirects. Also be aware that Amazon reduced item-level reporting detail in 2026, which makes it harder to verify a single sale by eye. When the report gets less granular, mistakes and underpayments hide more easily — which is exactly why a systematic audit beats eyeballing a dashboard.

Seasonal vs. structural: know the difference

Every Amazon affiliate sees seasonal dips — Q1 after the holidays, slow summers in some niches. The key difference: seasonal dips drop both clicks and earnings proportionally, so EPC stays roughly flat. A structural problem (rate cut, drift, tracking) drops earnings without dropping clicks, so EPC falls. If clicks look normal and money doesn't, stop assuming it's seasonal.

  1. Pull your Product Earnings Report as a CSV.

  4. If nothing flags, it's likely seasonal or a genuine product-mix shift. If it flags, you have evidence to act on.

Frequently asked questions

What is earnings-per-click (EPC)?

EPC is your total Amazon affiliate commission divided by your number of clicks. It measures how much each click is worth and is the fastest way to spot a silent rate cut or tracking problem.

How do I know if my drop is seasonal?

Check your EPC. Seasonal dips reduce clicks and earnings together, keeping EPC roughly flat. If clicks stay level but EPC falls, the problem is structural.

Does Amazon pay on items I didn't link to?

Not since April 14, 2026. Amazon now pays onsite commission only on the same ASIN variant as the product you linked; other items in the cart no longer count toward your commission. See our guide on the halo commission change.
