Amazon Affiliate Profit per Sale: The Real Math

Free tool:Amazon Commission Calculator — calculate what any Amazon sale earns.

When you quote "a 6% commission" on a product page, you're describing a ceiling — not what actually lands in your account. Between the base price, the discounts Amazon applies at checkout, the returns that come back two weeks later, and the 2026 rate cuts, the real profit per sale is usually well below the headline figure. This guide walks through each deduction, shows you how to estimate your true per-sale profit, and gives you the tools to measure the gap between gross and paid commission.

Gross commission: what the rate actually multiplies

Amazon calculates commission on the eligible order amount for the items you referred — not the price you saw when you wrote the review. The practical deductions:

  • Coupons and promotional discounts are subtracted before commission is applied.
  • Gift-wrap and shipping charges are excluded from the commissionable amount.
  • Gift cards and similar payment methods can also reduce the eligible total in some marketplaces.

The formula is simple: Gross commission = eligible order amount × category rate. At a 6% rate, a $120 product discounted by a $10 coupon yields commission on $110: $6.60 gross, not $7.20. The commission calculator applies these rules per category and market so you don't have to hold the edge cases in your head.

Net profit: returns and reversals

Gross commission is what a sale is worth the day it happens. Net profit is what survives to payday, and three things eat into it:

  • Returns. When an order is returned, the commission is reversed in a later reporting period. High-return categories — clothing, shoes, some home items — quietly cost you more than their rate suggests.
  • Fee reversals and adjustments. Price changes after referral, or order corrections, can pull commission back even without a full return.
  • Threshold and holding quirks. Because reversals hit in the month the return happens, a heavy-return month can make your paid commission look far lower than your gross commission for the same orders.

A realistic planning rule: discount gross commission by 20–40% for products in high-return categories, and by a smaller amount for low-return durable goods. In other words, that $6.60 gross sale might realistically net $4.50–$5.50. If you're trying to decide whether a category is worth your time, run the break-even calculator to see how many net sales cover your content costs before the first dollar of profit.

Estimating profit per sale for planning

Put the whole chain together for a product you're considering covering:

  1. Find the current rate for the category and market in the current rate table.
  2. Apply the expected price after typical discounts — not the list price.
  3. Multiply for gross commission. Enter both into the calculator for the exact figure.
  4. Apply your return discount for the category to reach a net planning figure.

Worked example: a $250 outdoor chair at a 6% home rate with a $25 coupon → $225 eligible × 6% = $13.50 gross. Outdoor furniture is a low-return category, so a 15% haircut leaves about $11.50 net per sale. Compare that against a $40 dress at 5% with a 40% return rate — gross $2.00, net closer to $1.20. Same effort, wildly different per-sale value. That comparison is the real reason to run the numbers instead of trusting the rate alone.

Checking what you actually got paid

Estimated profit is only useful if it matches reality. Amazon's 2026 reporting changes reduced item-level detail, which makes manual reconciliation harder than ever. Frequently asked questions

Is commission based on list price or sale price?

Commission is based on the sale price — the eligible order amount after discounts, coupons, and gift-wrap fees are excluded.

How do returns affect my commission?

When a referred order is returned, Amazon reverses the commission in a later period, so paid commission can run below gross. High-return categories cost you more per sale than their rate suggests.

What is a realistic profit margin per sale?

A realistic planning figure is 60% to 80% of gross commission for most categories, and less for high-return or heavily discounted products.
