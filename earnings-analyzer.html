Why analyze?

Understand your earnings report before you audit it

The Earnings Analyzer helps you read your Amazon Product Earnings Report. It shows totals by category, how many sales vs returns, your average commission per sale, and which rows it couldn't match to a known category.

The Earnings Audit does something different: it compares what you were paid against the applicable commission rate for each line item and flags possible discrepancies.

Recommended flow: Run the analyzer first to understand your data, then the audit to verify it.
How it works

What the AMZLOSS Earnings Analyzer does

  1. Reads the CSV in your browser — no upload, no server, no account.
  2. Auto-detects columns — finds Date, Category, Item Price, Quantity, Referral Fee, Rate regardless of exact header wording.
  3. Groups by category — uses the 2026 Amazon Associates rate table to label each row.
  4. Shows returns separately — negative prices/commissions are counted as returns.
  5. Flags unknown categories — rows that don't match a known Amazon category are listed for review.
Frequently asked

Earnings Analyzer questions

What is an Amazon Product Earnings Report?

It is a CSV file you can download from Amazon Associates that shows every commissionable transaction: product, category, sale price, quantity, referral fee (commission), rate applied, and date. The AMZLOSS analyzer reads that file and summarizes it for you.

How is the analyzer different from the earnings audit?

The analyzer helps you understand your report — totals by category, return rates, commission averages. The audit compares what you were paid against the applicable commission rate for each line item and flags possible discrepancies. Run the analyzer first to understand your data, then the audit to verify it.

Do you store my earnings data?

No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser. We never upload, store, or see your numbers, and there is no account or sign-up required.

What file format do I need?

Download your Product Earnings Report from Amazon Associates as a CSV. The analyzer accepts the standard Amazon CSV format with headers like Date, Product Title, Category, Item Price, Quantity, Referral Fee, and Rate.

Why are some rows "uncategorized"?

Amazon occasionally uses category names that don't match the published rate table (e.g., custom categories, marketplace-specific names, or typos). The analyzer lists them so you can review manually.
